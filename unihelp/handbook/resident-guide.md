# Juniper Court resident handbook

This handbook describes the fictional UniHelp demo. Staff use the approved rules and the job record to confirm repair targets.

## reporting-repairs: Reporting repairs

Give the exact room or shared area and describe the affected fitting. Explain what stopped working and when it started. If you have two separate faults, describe each one separately so staff can track the work. Keep the original report if one fault needs urgent attention.

## repair-targets: Repair targets

For fixed lighting, another working fixed light in the same room means P3 with a 7 day target. If no fixed light works in that room, use P2 with a 1 day target from when that fact was first confirmed. A torch, desk lamp or working light in another room does not count. Staff may ask you to confirm whether a second fixed light works.

Verified heating faults have a P2, 2 day target. Verified appliance faults have a P3, 7 day target. These targets guide repairs. The confirmed job record shows the target for your repair.

## chasing-repairs: Chasing an existing repair

Include the job number if you have it and say which fitting you mean. If more than one repair could match, staff may ask which job you mean. The recorded target is the point of reference for your follow-up. After that time passes, staff can review an open repair for escalation. Report any new hazard immediately, even if you already have a job number.

## keys-and-access: Keys and access

If you cannot get into your room or flat now, contact on-duty residence staff for identity and access checks. A lost key with a room identifier, a door that cannot be secured, or a door that traps someone inside also needs urgent attention. A request for a spare key is routine if you can still get in.

## water-leaks: Water leaks

Tell staff whether a tap or shower is only dripping into its fitting or whether water is spreading. A contained drip has a P3, 14 day target. Uncontrolled water, a ceiling leak or water reaching an electrical fitting needs urgent staff assessment.

## urgent-hazards: Urgent hazards

Contact the duty team promptly about loss of power to the whole flat, signs of electrical danger, a dark shared stairwell, unsafe shared routes, broken glass on a shared route, structural danger or someone trapped in a lift. A single fitting that stops working without signs of danger can usually be reviewed as a repair. Say what you can see and which area is affected.

## fire-and-smoke: Fire, smoke and alarms

Follow the building's emergency instructions and contact the fire and rescue service for active fire, smoke or a sounding alarm when staff have not confirmed a drill. Report a faulty alarm, an obstructed escape route or a damaged fire door to on-duty staff urgently, even if there is no active fire.

## gas-and-carbon-monoxide: Gas and carbon monoxide

Treat suspected gas leaks and carbon monoxide alarms as emergencies. Follow the building's emergency instructions and contact the National Gas Emergency Service from a safe place. Tell the emergency operator if anyone is in immediate danger.

## contacting-staff: Contacting staff and raising complaints

Use your residence's published contact details. If reception is closed, use the published out-of-hours contact. For an unresolved maintenance complaint, contact your building manager with the original report and any job number.

## quiet-hours: Quiet hours and shared spaces

Use the quiet hours posted in your building and keep shared routes clear. Contact reception if you cannot find the posted hours or need to report a recurring disturbance. Give the location and approximate time.
